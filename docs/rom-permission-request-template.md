# Homebrew ROM — commercial-bundling permission request

A reusable email to get **written permission** to bundle a homebrew game in
Fullstack Wolfpack (a paid, closed-source app). A clear "yes" here turns a
freeware title into a **clear-for-you** title — the highest-yield way to build a
bundle roster (see `rom-licensing.md`).

**How to use**
- Fill the `<PLACEHOLDERS>`. Send from a real address you control.
- The reply is your grant — **keep every reply on file** (archive the thread /
  save a PDF). Note the date + exact wording.
- Watch the **asset/music carve-out** in §3 — that's where rights usually break
  (a dev often wrote the code but licensed music from someone else). If they
  can't clear the music, don't bundle that build.
- Get the "yes" **before** you ship. No reply = no permission.

## Full version

**Subject:** Permission to include *<GAME TITLE>* in Fullstack Wolfpack?

> Hi <AUTHOR NAME>,
>
> I've been playing *<GAME TITLE>* and it's genuinely great — <ONE SPECIFIC,
> HONEST DETAIL YOU LIKED>. Thank you for making it.
>
> I'm building **Fullstack Wolfpack**, a coding-education app with a small retro
> arcade users can play between lessons (<APP URL>). It's a **commercial (paid),
> closed-source** product across web, iOS/Android, and desktop.
>
> I'd love to **include *<GAME TITLE>* as a bundled, ready-to-play title**, with
> **prominent credit to you** (your name + a link of your choosing) shown in the
> app. I'm writing because I want this done right — with your permission, in
> writing — rather than assuming.
>
> **What I'm asking for:** a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free permission to
> **reproduce and distribute the *<GAME TITLE>* ROM as part of Fullstack
> Wolfpack**, including in the **paid, closed-source** app and across web, mobile,
> and desktop versions. I'm **not** asking to modify the game, claim authorship,
> or resell it standalone — just to include it so players can enjoy it in-app,
> with credit to you.
>
> **One thing to confirm:** do you hold (or can you grant) the rights to the
> **whole game — code, art, music, and sound effects**? If any part (e.g. the
> music) is someone else's or under a non-commercial license, just let me know
> and I'll sort that out or leave that piece out.
>
> If you're happy with this, a reply saying **"I grant Fullstack Wolfpack
> permission as described"** is all I need. If you'd prefer different terms —
> specific attribution wording, a fee, a free lifetime Pro account, or any limits
> — I'm glad to work with whatever suits you.
>
> Either way, thanks for the game.
>
> Best,
> <YOUR NAME>
> <YOUR ROLE / “developer, Fullstack Wolfpack”>
> <CONTACT / APP URL>

## Short version (for quick DMs / itch.io / forums)

> Hi <AUTHOR NAME> — love *<GAME TITLE>*! I make **Fullstack Wolfpack**, a paid
> coding-learning app with an in-app retro arcade (<APP URL>). Could I **bundle
> *<GAME TITLE>* in it, with prominent credit to you**? It's commercial and
> closed-source, on web/mobile/desktop — non-exclusive, no changes to your game,
> just letting players enjoy it in-app. Also: do you hold the rights to the whole
> game incl. music/art? A "yes, you may bundle it as described" is all I need —
> or name your terms. Thank you!

## If they say yes — capture this

Log it (a `THIRD-PARTY.md` / permissions folder in the repo, or a spreadsheet):

- Title, author, date of the grant
- The exact permission wording they replied with
- Attribution text + link they want shown in-app
- Any carve-outs (e.g. "music is CC-BY-NC — excluded") or terms (fee, comp account)
- Link to the archived thread / saved PDF

> Not legal advice — but a clear written grant on file is exactly what makes a
> bundled title defensible. For a commercial launch, have counsel skim the
> collected grants alongside the core-licensing plan in `rom-licensing.md`.
